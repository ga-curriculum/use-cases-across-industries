
# Use Cases Across Industries: Instructor Guide

**Module Time:** 120 minutes  

---

## What This Module Is About

This module transitions learners from **agent architecture theory** to **real-world business application**.

Up to this point, students have learned:

- What agents are  
- How they are architected  
- How reasoning, memory, and tools interact  
- How autonomy levels differ  

This session answers a new question:

> Where do agents create measurable business value — and what determines whether they succeed or fail?

The goal is not to memorize use cases.  
The goal is to teach learners to **analyze any business process and identify where an agent could participate safely and effectively.**

---

## Core Objectives

By the end of this module, learners should be able to:

- Identify high-impact agentic applications across industries  
- Classify use cases using autonomy and business value frameworks  
- Analyze governance implications of different autonomy levels  
- Deconstruct agent-process integration step by step  
- Recognize architectural implications hidden inside business descriptions  

---

## Must-Hit Topics

- Industry context shapes architecture  
- Agents create outcomes, not just answers  
- Autonomy is a design decision  
- Governance scales with autonomy  
- Agents integrate into processes — they do not replace them  
- Process deconstruction reveals hidden architectural requirements  

---

# 1. Real-World Applications Across Industries  
**Time:** ~ 15 minutes

## Purpose

Expose learners to concrete, cross-industry implementations and reinforce how **architecture maps to business outcomes**.

## Key Talking Points

For each industry, reinforce:

- What the business problem is  
- Which agent components are doing the heavy lifting  
- What constraint defines the deployment boundary  
- Why autonomy level matters  

## Critical Insight to Emphasize

The same agent loop behaves differently in:

- Healthcare → compliance-driven  
- Finance → audit-driven  
- Customer service → escalation-driven  
- HR → bias-sensitive  
- Logistics → threshold-controlled  
- Marketing → budget-guarded  

Architecture must reflect industry risk profile.

---

# 2. Classifying Use Cases by Autonomy and Business Value  
**Time:** ~ 15 minutes

## Purpose

Provide a structured decision-making framework that prevents naive deployment.

## Core Teaching Principle

> Autonomy without governance is liability.  
> Autonomy with measurement is leverage.

## Critical Insight

Higher autonomy + higher business transformation  
= higher governance investment required.

Autonomy ↑  →  Risk ↑  →  Governance ↑

## Teaching Reminder

Reinforce the principle:

> Start supervised. Earn autonomy.

---

# 3. Deconstructing Agent-Process Interactions  
**Time:** ~15 minutes

## Purpose

Move from conceptual use cases to **architectural specificity**.

## Core Insight

High-level descriptions hide engineering complexity.

Deconstruction reveals:

- Required tool surface  
- Data dependencies  
- Policy embedding requirements  
- Failure-handling logic  
- Logging obligations  

## Critical Observation

A business description hides at least 10 architectural decisions.
